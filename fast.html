Avoiding hotel queues with TOTVS Fast Check‑in

As the only designer on the product, I turned a little-used, desktop-only pre-check-in into one of the most valued features of TOTVS PMS — and then scaled it into a white-label product.

Product Design
Mobile-first
Data-informed
White label
DataDog
Context

In Brazil, every hotel guest must fill in the FNRH (Ficha Nacional de Registro de Hóspedes), a mandatory registration form defined by Decree 7.381/2010. At the front desk this means personal data, documents and a signature for every guest, every stay — and long queues at peak times.

TOTVS Fast Check-in already existed to solve this: guests fill in the FNRH before arriving and only show their ID at the desk. But very few guests were completing it.

The problem

At the start, the only data we had was the number of forms submitted — we couldn't see where or why guests gave up. So we contacted our clients, the hotels, to understand why they weren’t using it, and I reviewed the flow end to end. Three problems came up:

  • 01Usability and development flaws in the flow itself. The most critical: the form wasn’t sent to guests automatically — staff had to send it one by one, and the action to do it was buried inside other flows.
  • 02Weak integration with the PMS, so the front desk had little reason to trust and promote it.
  • 03Unusable on mobile — the flow got stuck and guests couldn’t finish it, even though that’s where guests open the link.

This wasn’t only a UI problem. To be used, the feature had to work on the device guests actually have — and deliver data the front desk could trust.

Making it measurable

“Forms submitted” couldn’t tell us what was going wrong. So, alongside the redesign, I pushed to instrument the flow with DataDog — from then on every decision could be checked step by step instead of guessed, and the team started following the numbers together.

Key design decisions
4.1

Mobile-first, not “desktop made responsive”

Guests open the link on their phones, often on the way to the hotel. I rebuilt the flow around short steps with one main action each.

DISCARDED → reusing the existing components as they were. Several of them simply didn’t work on mobile, so I redesigned or replaced them.

4.2

Two signature experiences, negotiated with the devs

The signature is legally required, and signing with a finger is a different gesture from signing with a mouse. Instead of forcing one component onto every screen, I worked with the developers to build two versions of the signature capture — one for mobile, one for web. On the phone, the guest is asked to turn it sideways, which gives a wide signing area; on the web, signing happens in a dedicated modal.

TRADE-OFF → more development and maintenance effort, accepted so each device gets a signature that actually works.

4.3

Explaining why we ask for so much data

Asking for documents before arrival feels invasive. I added a short explanation of what the FNRH is and that it’s required by law (“What is the guest form?”), right where the doubt appears — not hidden in a FAQ.

4.4

One booking, several guests — with visible status

A reservation usually has more than one guest. The booker sees every guest with a clear status (Completed / Signature pending) and can edit or finish each one.

4.5

A check-in card that closes the loop

At the end, the guest gets a check-in card with dates and a code to show at the desk: the guest knows they’re done, and staff find the record in the PMS right away.

Results

Fast Check-in became one of the most valued features of TOTVS PMS and was adopted by major hotel chains.

Scaling: white label

Success created a new demand: more hotels wanted to offer Fast Check-in, but under their own brand. I designed a customizable white-label version, so each hotel can apply its identity without breaking the flow and the usability we had already validated.

In a settings panel, the hotel sets its name, cover image, logo, font and five brand colors — with a live phone preview. Only brand choices are configurable; the layout and components stay fixed, so every hotel’s version keeps the validated flow. The same panel also manages data collection, the guest profile, an add-ons module and extra offers.

Next: guest profile
Concept

I also conceived an integration with the guest profile in the PMS: using the pre-check-in moment to collect optional extra information, so hotels can offer personalized service and a better stay. It turns a legal obligation into the starting point of the guest relationship.

The hotel picks and reorders the questions it wants to ask — dietary restrictions, special requests, bed or floor preference — and turns each one on or off, again with a live preview.

What I learned

If you can’t measure it, make it measurable

Starting with only “forms submitted”, instrumenting the flow was as important as redesigning it.

Mobile-first means rethinking, not shrinking

The signature showed that sometimes the right answer is two solutions, not one compromise.

Good features become products

Growth brought new needs — brand customization and personalization — to design for without losing what made it work.
